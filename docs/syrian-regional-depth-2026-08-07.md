# Syrian regional depth, release 1.13.0

Last reviewed: 2026-08-07

## Outcome

Release 1.13.0 adds 86 bilingual, source-bound regional-depth identities and
one separate pomegranate-concentrate product identity to the existing Syrian
foundation. The Syrian graph grows from 109 to 196 identities and the complete
culinary-science registry grows from 200 to 287 identities.

The current Syrian graph contains:

- 56 dishes
- 55 ingredients
- 21 regional or topic hubs
- 17 techniques
- 17 traditions
- 15 preparations
- 4 markets
- 2 restaurants
- 2 hospitality institutions
- 3 private retail or market observations
- 1 cuisine owner

Entity Studio consequently resolves 343 subjects: 287 science identities plus
56 product identities. Product price-basis coverage remains 56 of 56, composed
of 36 live WooCommerce prices and 20 private planning prices. The 87 new
Syrian identities create no WooCommerce offer, stock row, supplier claim,
planning price or payment change.

## Regional ownership

The graph now distinguishes:

- Aleppo and its kibbeh and sour-fruit families
- Damascus, rural Damascus, Qamar al-Din, booza and Al-Mrah rose practices
- Homs and its liquid-cooked kibbeh family, stuffed carrots and sweets
- Hama and its stuffed leaves, shakriyeh and offal preparation identities
- Idlib and Maarrat al-Numan
- Afrin as a Kurdish and northern Syrian context
- Latakia, Baniyas and Jableh under a Syrian coastal parent
- Jazira and explicitly sourced Assyrian, Kurdish and Arab contexts
- Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor as separate Euphrates owners
- Palmyra as separate from Deir ez-Zor
- Qamishli family transmission, explicitly separated from direct city evidence
- Suwayda, Hauran and shared southern Syrian institutions

## Community ownership

Syrian Jewish foodways remain a constituent tradition under Syrian cuisine.
Aleppan and Damascene Jewish records retain separate owners. Family adaptations
for Passover, Shabbat, weekdays and migration remain family or community
records unless another source establishes broader usage.

Assyrian Dikhwa, the rural Alawite coastal account, Kurdish and Arab Jazira
labels, and Druze or shared southern practices retain their exact source scope.
No community is used as a substitute for Syrian cuisine as a whole.

## Canonical decisions

- Halawet al-jibn keeps one owner with Homs and Hama variant sections. Competing
  origin testimony is not resolved editorially.
- Syrian coastal sayyadiyah keeps one owner with Tartus and Latakia variants.
- Mlihi keeps one Hauran owner with Daraa and Suwayda facets.
- Aleppine fried kibbeh names remain one comparison owner until their separate
  consumer intent and formulas are proven.
- Palmyrene burma remains separate from an Aleppine kibbeh carrying a similar
  name.
- Qaren yaruq owns the documented Deir ez-Zor variant, not the wider
  cross-border dish family.
- Kutilk, shamburak, boraniyeh and kulki remain identity-review records where
  the current evidence does not justify a final formula.
- The observed Keter Harimon pomegranate concentrate is owned by its exact
  retail-listing identity. Pomegranate molasses is linked for comparison only,
  and the observation remains non-comparable until the exact label and formula
  establish equivalence.

Commercial cross-sells are limited to product-shaped ingredient, equipment or
material identities. A culinary `requires` edge may point to a technique, but a
technique is never projected as a product merely because another entity needs
it.

## Science boundary

Every new food identity states what its source actually identifies and what it
does not measure. No pH, Brix, water activity, temperature curve, shelf life,
medical benefit or molecular concentration is invented. Dried dairy, meat in
fat, offal, overnight dairy, raw-meat history and preserved foods remain without
public process instructions until the relevant food-safety and kitchen tests
exist.

## Publication boundary

The public Syrian gateway remains bilingual and `noindex,follow`. The other 195
Syrian identities remain private. Every new identity has:

- one stable owner and parent path
- Hebrew and English names, summary and search intent
- one or more source-bound claims
- linked ingredients or contextual entities where evidence supports them
- a commercial culinary image prompt
- private, noindex, reference-only publication and commerce state
- no WooCommerce product code and no public offer

## Work remaining before the next cuisine

The Syrian knowledge graph is now broad enough to begin Lebanon without losing
the Syrian regional structure. Syrian public activation still requires a
separate quality wave rather than more indiscriminate entity creation.

The remaining Syrian activation work is estimated as:

1. Corroborate about 20 thin or single-household identities with a second source
   or a named regional reviewer.
2. Kitchen-test the first 12 high-intent dishes, beginning with kebab bil karaz,
   kibbeh safarjaliyyeh, kibbeh meshwiyyeh, muhammara, harraq isbao, Damascene
   fatteh, yabraq and yalangi, shish barak, Damascene booza, Qamar al-Din drink
   and Homs al-mughtuta.
3. Produce and clear the first 20 original hero and process images.
4. Complete allergen, yield, storage and food-safety records for every tested
   recipe.
5. Promote only the pages with unique consumer intent, complete bilingual copy,
   useful internal links, visible sources and matching schema.

This is one activation wave, not another foundational mapping wave. Lebanon can
start from the same ontology now. Iraq should follow as a separate country hub,
with Baghdad, Mosul, Basra, the Euphrates-Tigris food systems, Iraqi Jewish
foodways and Kurdish Iraqi traditions kept as distinct owners.
