# Complete99 consumer culinary and ecommerce benchmark

Date: 2026-07-29  
Status: evidence-based product and experience benchmark, not a release claim  
Scope: current Complete99 public WordPress site compared with Hummus Ashkara, Delitlv, R2M, Sweetgreen, Ottolenghi, Eataly, and Farmer J

Update: [2026-07-31 benchmark and gap-map addendum](consumer-culinary-ecommerce-benchmark-addendum-2026-07-31.md)

## Executive decision

Complete99's public website must address culinary consumers only. Its current bilingual technical foundation, distinctive visual system, food-led mobile hero, controlled SEO behavior, and live-browser accessibility evidence are useful assets, but the public positioning does not yet honor that audience boundary and does not yet offer a competitive consumer culinary or ecommerce journey.

All institutional, workforce, task, inventory, supplier, cost, campaign, and operations matters belong exclusively in the authenticated private Complete99 OS. They must not appear in the public navigation, page hierarchy, body copy, calls to action, illustrations, structured data, search ownership, sitemap, or indexable routes. The public site owns dishes, recipes, ingredients, traditions, culinary guides, verified consumer locations, and real consumer commerce only when each surface is ready.

That gap is currently truthful, not a deployment defect:

- The live public catalog contained one published branch record, zero menu sections, zero public items, and zero public campaigns at the review time.
- The dish hub therefore shows an honest empty state instead of stale or estimated menu content.
- The future store page is `noindex` and explicitly has no products, prices, stock, cart, payment, or delivery promise.
- The future canonical domain is not connected. The UPress alias remains the live canonical base.

The immediate priority is to enforce the public-private boundary, then make the public site food-specific, useful, and human while the first culinary records complete their source, kitchen, allergen, image, and editorial gates. Commerce should remain held until merchant, product, tax, payment, fulfilment, policy, support, and test-order evidence exists.

The best model for Complete99 is:

1. Hummus Ashkara's immediate local food clarity.
2. Delitlv's Hebrew product hierarchy and product-detail completeness.
3. R2M's hospitality atmosphere and local ecosystem confidence.
4. Sweetgreen's structured menu, ingredient, allergen, nutrition, and location paths.
5. Ottolenghi's recipe, ingredient, editorial, and shop graph.
6. Eataly's category, product, search, and store architecture.
7. Farmer J's recognizable illustration system and mobile food-first action.

Complete99 should not copy their first-load cookie walls, offer walls, carousel dependence, sprawling menus, unsupported heritage claims, or transaction controls before a real consumer offer is approved.

## Scope and scoring method

This review used:

- live Complete99 pages and the public-catalog endpoint on 2026-07-29;
- Complete99 release, SEO, editorial, media-rights, and Chrome-acceptance evidence;
- supplied desktop and mobile screenshots captured on 2026-07-28 and 2026-07-29;
- current public pages from the seven named competitors;
- read-only inspection only. No form, cart, checkout, account, or order was submitted.

Scores are directional UX scores, not certifications:

- `0`: absent or intentionally held
- `1`: weak
- `2`: partial or fragmented
- `3`: competent
- `4`: strong
- `5`: category-leading in the reviewed evidence

The accessibility score reflects observable interface and supplied test evidence. It is not a WCAG conformance claim or legal accessibility opinion. For AI-tell risk, `Low` is better. The label describes the risk that a visitor may perceive templated or synthetic communication. It does not assert how any competitor's content was produced.

## What each benchmark teaches

### Hummus Ashkara: local intent in one screen

Observed strengths:

- The hero names the food category and the local destination immediately.
- Food photography occupies substantial visual space.
- Menu, delivery partners, phone, address, and hours are close to the primary decision.
- The site supports Hebrew and English without turning language choice into a separate journey.
- Story, menu, and local trust reinforce one another rather than competing.

Useful Complete99 adaptation:

- When a real location and current offer are verified, place the location, service status, hours, and next action together.
- Use one strong food subject rather than a general collage when a page owns a specific dish or menu intent.
- Keep the consumer action literal, such as viewing an approved dish or contacting a verified location.

Do not copy:

- Heritage, kosher, hours, address, availability, or delivery claims without current owned evidence.
- Third-party ordering buttons until the destination, account ownership, service area, and failure owner are verified.

### Delitlv: the strongest local commerce reference

Observed strengths:

- Hebrew ecommerce taxonomy is extensive and task-oriented.
- Category pages expose filters, product counts, sorting, product descriptions, prices, units, and direct add-to-cart actions.
- The reviewed PDP includes breadcrumb context, product photography, quantity, price, unit size, SKU, orderable dates, heating guidance, allergens, ingredients, nutrition, recommendations, and cart action.
- The order journey asks for delivery time and method before promising fulfilment.
- Stores, contact, questions, cancellation, privacy, terms, and accessibility are present.
- Editorial magazine content gives products a broader culinary context.

Useful Complete99 adaptation:

- Model products and future prepared-food items with explicit IDs, variants, units, dates, ingredients, allergens, storage or heating information, and fulfilment constraints.
- Require delivery context before claiming availability.
- Build the category graph around consumer tasks and food use, not internal sourcing structures.
- Link a real product to editorial context without merging the product and ingredient intents.

Do not copy:

- The full category depth before Complete99 has enough real products to justify it.
- A very large mega menu that exposes every future category.
- Newsletter, club, or promotional interruption before a visitor can understand the offer.

### R2M: atmosphere and ecosystem confidence

Observed strengths:

- Real hospitality photography communicates atmosphere before explanation.
- The group story connects restaurants, bakery, deli, makers, B2B, and gift activity.
- Named places and contact details create local substance.
- The brand voice feels authored and place-specific.

Useful Complete99 adaptation:

- Show kitchens, tables, dishes, hands, and service moments once rights and context are approved.
- Use the About page to tell a specific, sourced story about food, people, craft, and place.
- Add a real local proof layer only when location and contact records pass review.

Do not copy:

- Group-centric storytelling that leaves a first-time visitor unsure which action to take.
- Commerce split across multiple domains unless there is a clear handoff and support owner.

### Sweetgreen: menu data as consumer trust

Observed strengths:

- The main navigation makes menu, catering, locations, and ordering distinct.
- Menu categories are readable and crawlable.
- Items expose ingredients, allergen markers, calories, and macronutrient information.
- Location pages combine local ordering and a location-specific menu.
- A visible operational notice responds to a current supply-chain concern instead of hiding it.

Useful Complete99 adaptation:

- Treat every approved dish as structured data with a stable identity and controlled fields.
- Keep item name, image, short description, ingredient summary, allergens, current status, and next action together.
- Create location-specific availability only after a genuine location record exists.
- Separate editorial recipes from the current menu and from commercial products.

Do not copy:

- Nutrition, certification, sourcing, or sustainability claims without source ownership and review.
- Large issue banners that displace the menu when a smaller accessible notice would work.
- Privacy UI that covers most of a mobile screen.

### Ottolenghi: the best content-commerce graph

Observed strengths:

- Recipes, restaurants, products, ingredients, Foodipedia, catering, and locations share one navigation system.
- Recipe discovery supports course, collection, main ingredient, season, and occasion.
- Product categories are understandable without exposing every SKU in the top navigation.
- The reviewed PDP connects product story, contents, storage, allergy guidance, delivery, related products, and related recipes.
- The cart preserves editorial discovery instead of becoming a dead end.
- Named founders, chefs, locations, and a test-kitchen story add human accountability.

Useful Complete99 adaptation:

- Build the graph `dish -> ingredient -> tradition -> guide -> real product`, with one canonical intent owner at each step.
- Let ingredient pages support both culinary discovery and future product selection without duplicating product intent.
- Give empty states a useful continuation, but do not fill them with unrelated products before commerce is live.
- Make authors, testers, editors, dates, and sources visible on approved culinary content.

Do not copy:

- The first-load consent dialog that covers the mobile hero.
- The full taxonomy before enough substantive pages exist.
- Product availability labels without stock and fulfilment evidence.

### Eataly: retail scale with editorial category landings

Observed strengths:

- Search, category, account, cart, stores, and help are visible in the main chrome.
- Grocery categories lead to real product grids.
- Product cards show quantity, unit pricing, price, and add-to-cart.
- The reviewed PDP includes producer, availability, quantity, unit price, description, label and detail tabs, cart action, payment indicators, and service links.
- Category introductions connect products to a culinary theme.
- Store pages and corporate information strengthen an international-local network.

Useful Complete99 adaptation:

- Use WooCommerce categories, attributes, variations, and product identifiers when commerce is ready.
- Show unit pricing and product facts consistently with the visible offer and structured data.
- Create edited category landings rather than thin filter archives.
- Keep search, cart, and account controls hidden until they have a truthful function.

Do not copy:

- Product counts as a substitute for category usefulness.
- Facet and category sprawl that creates thin crawlable combinations.
- Large consent panels over the primary content.

### Farmer J: distinctive brand behavior on mobile

Observed strengths:

- Illustration is recognizable without looking like generic software art.
- The mobile hero is food-first, with a clear click-and-collect action.
- Order, menu, visit, recipe, and shop paths are easy to distinguish.
- Language or market selection is visible.
- Menu and allergen resources provide practical continuation.

Useful Complete99 adaptation:

- Develop a repeatable illustration language for concepts that cannot use documentary photography.
- Pair illustration with real food photography, not instead of food.
- Keep one literal mobile action visible after the header.

Do not copy:

- Carousel dependence.
- A mixed market state where one country offers ordering and another says it is coming soon without a clear regional boundary.
- Cookie UI that occupies a large portion of the first mobile screen.

## Exact Complete99 current-state findings

### What is already strong

1. **Bilingual and crawlable architecture**

   The 1.2.1 acceptance evidence confirms 30 bilingual public-discovery routes, one H1 per page, correct language and direction, exact canonical URLs, reciprocal `he`, `en`, and `x-default` alternates, parseable `WebPage` structured data, and 62 working surfaced internal links.

2. **Mobile and keyboard evidence**

   The reviewed Complete99 mobile capture gives food the first large visual position, keeps the brand and menu available, and stacks both actions clearly. Live Chrome acceptance found no horizontal overflow, no visible target below 44 CSS pixels, no console errors, Escape closing for the mobile menu, body-scroll lock, and focus return.

3. **Fail-closed transaction boundary**

   `/store/` is `noindex`, absent from the sitemap, and contains no cart or checkout. No products, prices, stock, payment, or delivery promise are exposed while the consumer offer is incomplete.

4. **Truthful menu boundary**

   The public read model was fresh and contained zero public menu items. The site did not restore old or estimated items. Unknown menu routes return a localized 404 with `noindex` and recovery links.

5. **Distinct visual identity**

   The green, cream, lime, bold Hebrew display type, compact mark, rounded control language, and archive-food palette are recognizable. The site does not look like a stock SaaS template.

### Exact gaps

1. **The public audience boundary is violated**

   The reviewed home page and desktop evidence foreground foodservice, procurement, workplace, workforce, and operating-system concepts. Public routes for services, industries, platform capability, and proposal intent reinforce that positioning. Under the governing direction, these are not secondary public audiences or supporting public content. They are private-system matters and must leave the public header, footer, body copy, calls to action, illustrations, sitemap, index ownership, and crawlable route graph.

2. **The culinary CTA currently leads to an empty value proposition**

   The home page promotes a dish library, but the authoritative catalog contains no menu sections and no items. The dish hub explains what future dossiers will contain, then shows no dish to discover. This is truthful but weak for a consumer.

3. **The first desktop screen gives more area to broad brand language than to food**

   The food image is attractive but smaller than the headline block. Hummus Ashkara, Ottolenghi, Eataly, and Farmer J make the food or retail environment the dominant visual evidence.

4. **The site shifts from food to private-system simulation**

   A large interface illustration and abstract management cards appear before a visitor receives a specific dish, ingredient, method, cook, place, or culinary proof. This is not only a pacing problem. It exposes concepts that belong in the private Complete99 OS and conflicts with the consumer-only public role.

5. **The current public catalog has a branch record without a complete public local journey**

   The endpoint exposes one branch marked published and active, but the reviewed consumer surface does not provide a verified location page with a complete NAP record, hours, contact route, service facts, and local menu. The API record alone must not be treated as public trust proof.

6. **There is no product layer**

   There are no PLPs, PDPs, product identifiers, variants, prices, consumer availability states, shipping facts, cart, checkout, order confirmation, or customer-support path. This is expected under the current hold.

7. **The culinary hubs describe governance more than food**

   Dishes, ingredients, traditions, knowledge, and store pages contain substantial explanations of what pages will eventually do. They contain little current, named, sensory, tested, dated, or source-linked culinary material for a reader to use now.

8. **The brand domain is not ready for consumer trust or long-term SEO**

   The live canonical base remains the UPress alias. The future `complete99.co.il` domain is not connected. Serious branded search, local citations, merchant feeds, and scaled consumer acquisition should wait for the real domain, redirects, TLS, and canonical migration evidence.

9. **Image evidence is narrow**

   The live release references 13 optimized image assets, but the media-rights register limits archive photography to general culinary illustration. It may not prove current menu availability, a branch, a customer, a certification, a preparation method, or a product offer. Paid media and product use require further rights evidence.

10. **Repeated modules create a high AI-tell risk**

   The risk comes from visible patterns, not a claim about authorship:

   - repeated headings such as responsibility boundaries and next steps across unrelated hubs;
   - repeated numbered card grids with abstract descriptions;
   - future-tense copy explaining what a library will contain;
   - repeated phrases about one connected system, which also breach the public-private boundary;
   - large blocks of process language without names, dates, dishes, ingredients, tested methods, sources, or first-person expertise;
   - empty gallery shapes and simulation UI where a consumer expects food evidence.

## Journey analysis

### Public-private boundary

The current paths through Services, Industries, Platform, and Fit review are not public consumer journeys to optimize. They should be removed from public navigation and search ownership, then moved into the authenticated private system if they remain needed there. The public website must not provide a discovery or conversion funnel for institutions, workers, internal management, or procurement.

### Culinary reader

Current path:

`Home -> Dish library -> Empty state -> Knowledge hub`

Assessment:

- The navigation works.
- The destination does not yet answer a dish, ingredient, technique, tradition, or recipe question.
- The next useful milestone is one fully gated bilingual culinary record, not a larger empty taxonomy.
- The continuation should remain within culinary discovery, such as a related ingredient, recipe, tradition, or guide.

### Visitor seeking a current menu or local meal

Current path:

`Home -> Dish library -> No current public item`

Assessment:

- The site correctly refuses to imply a current menu.
- There is no verified order, pickup, delivery, hours, current price, or local-menu path.
- Do not add one until a location and offer are genuinely operational.

### Retail shopper

Current path:

`Home or footer -> Store architecture -> No products or transaction`

Assessment:

- The hold state is clear and `noindex`.
- The public architecture page exposes internal readiness concepts and should leave the public route graph.
- Reserve the store destination for launch only after at least one real category and product set is ready.

### P1: launch the first useful culinary graph

Do not bulk-publish. Launch the smallest complete graph only after every applicable gate passes.

Recommended first slice:

`one approved dish dossier -> its approved ingredients -> one sourced tradition context -> one practical knowledge guide`

For each published dish:

- stable ID, canonical path, locale pair, translation group, parent hub, and primary intent;
- rights-approved food image with explicit dimensions and useful alt text;
- concise identity, sensory description, ingredients, allergens, and current availability boundary;
- claim-level sources and bibliography;
- tested recipe, yield, scaling, kitchen notes, and sign-off where a recipe is public;
- author, chef tester, editor, reviewer, and dates;
- links to relevant ingredients, tradition, recipes, and culinary guides;
- `BreadcrumbList` plus only the schema supported by the visible facts;
- Recipe structured data only when the recipe and required fields are genuinely public;
- Menu or MenuItem facts only when a current menu status is approved;
- no price, stock, location, kosher, nutrition, delivery, or certification field without matching evidence.

Once enough real records exist, add:

- dish browsing by course or culinary question;
- ingredient browsing by use and technique;
- text search across approved entities;
- related-content modules based on owned relationships;
- a concise bilingual editorial standard page;
- visible source and update information.

Do not create filters that lead to empty or one-item pages merely to resemble a mature marketplace.

### P2: prepare consumer commerce behind the hold

WooCommerce is the intended store engine. The public store remains held and `noindex` until all required owners and evidence exist.

Required private launch readiness:

- merchant legal identity and a working consumer-support contact route;
- approved product records, IDs, variations, images, descriptions, units, prices, VAT treatment, and a private availability source;
- verified payment-provider account;
- shipping or pickup regions, rates, packaging, delivery windows, and failure handling;
- cancellation, returns, privacy, terms, accessibility, warranty, and customer-support ownership;
- test orders, failed payments, refunds, tax, email, availability synchronization, analytics, and security checks;
- visible Product and Offer data matching structured data exactly.

These readiness records remain private. The public product experience may expose only consumer facts needed to make an informed purchase, such as current availability, price, ingredients, allergens, fulfilment, policies, and support.

Recommended public flow after approval:

`Store -> Edited category landing -> Product detail -> Cart -> Delivery or pickup context -> Checkout -> Confirmation and support`

PDP minimum:

- product identity, responsible merchant where legally required, SKU, variation and unit;
- rights-approved images;
- visible price and VAT treatment;
- consumer availability from a real private source, without exposing internal quantities or controls;
- ingredients, allergens, storage, handling, and date constraints for food;
- dimensions, material, use, care, warranty, and safety documents for equipment;
- fulfilment estimate derived from the actual service;
- returns and support links;
- related guide or recipe only when the relationship is useful.

### P3: verified locations and local ordering

Only after a genuine location passes review:

- publish one location page with verified NAP, hours, service facts, access information, images, owner, and update date;
- connect location-specific menu availability;
- expose pickup or delivery only for supported areas;
- connect Google Business Profile and other local citations using the same NAP;
- use `LocalBusiness` schema only for visible, current facts;
- provide a working consumer contact route;
- create no doorway city pages.


## Copy and visual direction

### Hero

Keep:

- exact Hebrew brand;
- visible bilingual control;
- real food as the first visual proof.

Change:

- use one concise consumer culinary promise grounded in content the site actually contains;
- use a literal continuation such as Explore dishes, Discover ingredients, Read recipes, or Explore traditions only when the destination has approved value;
- make the food frame larger on desktop;
- shorten the headline or supporting sentence so the food and consumer decision share the first screen;
- remove small decorative chips that repeat information without advancing a journey.

Do not add:

- order, shop, delivery, menu, price, stock, branch, capacity, or response-time language before evidence exists;
- client logos, ratings, counters, certification marks, or customer quotes without permission and source ownership.
- platform, workforce, procurement, proposal, management, supplier, cost, campaign, or operational language in public copy.

### Culinary pages

Replace abstract system language with:

- the name of the dish, ingredient, person, place, or technique;
- what the reader can learn or do;
- sensory and process detail grounded in a tested or sourced record;
- visible authorship and review date;
- specific related links.

Avoid:

- identical four-card sections on every hub;
- numbered cards that do not represent a real sequence;
- repeated statements about future completeness;
- system, management, workflow, or integration language on public culinary pages;
- decorative interface panels on pages whose main purpose is food knowledge.

### Imagery

Use three distinct image classes:

1. Documentary food, craft, people, and place photography with recorded rights and context.
2. Product photography with exact SKU or item ownership.
3. Clearly labelled editorial illustration for conceptual material.

Do not use the generated connected-table illustration on the public website as either documentary proof or a product explanation. It depicts private-system concepts. Keep it only in private materials where its approved role is clearly labelled as editorial illustration.

## SEO priorities

### Current strengths

- Hebrew root and mirrored English hierarchy.
- Exact canonical and reciprocal hreflang acceptance.
- Controlled index policy and sitemap.
- Unknown menu routes fail with localized 404 and `noindex`.

### Current gaps

- No public dish, recipe, ingredient, tradition, location, category, or product entity provides consumer search value yet.
- Public institutional and private-system routes currently own crawlable intent that conflicts with the consumer-only website.
- The UPress alias is not the final brand domain.
- Hub copy is broad and explanatory, with limited current entity evidence.
- The store can describe architecture but cannot own transactional queries while no products exist.
- A branch API record is not enough to own local search intent.

### Exact SEO sequence

1. Remove public ownership for institutional, workforce, management, procurement, supplier, cost, campaign, and operations intent. Remove those URLs from navigation and the sitemap. Redirect only where a retired URL has a genuinely equivalent consumer destination; otherwise return a truthful `404` or `410`.
2. Keep the authenticated private system blocked from crawling and absent from public sitemaps, structured data, canonical graphs, and internal links.
3. Define one bilingual consumer intent registry for dishes, recipes, ingredients, traditions, guides, verified locations, and real products.
4. Complete the final-domain migration and preserve appropriate redirects, canonicals, hreflang, sitemap, and analytics.
5. Publish the first fully gated dish entity and its locale pair.
6. Add its supporting ingredient, recipe, tradition, and guide links only when substantive.
7. Validate breadcrumb and supported culinary schema against visible content.
8. Add a genuine consumer location only after NAP and ownership pass.
9. Add WooCommerce category and product entities only when the offers are real.
10. Keep thin filters, empty taxonomies, utility variants, private types, drafts, and held commerce out of the sitemap.
11. Measure branded, culinary, local, and product intents separately.

- sticky actions must not cover product or consent information;
- search, filters, quantity, cart, delivery slots, and checkout controls need visible labels;
- menus and dialogs need keyboard containment, Escape handling, and focus return;
- status, stock, price, error, and confirmation cannot rely on color alone;
- form errors need programmatic association and an error summary;
- RTL and LTR must preserve logical order in price, quantity, date, and address controls;
- product and recipe images need meaningful alt text, not keyword lists;
- motion and carousels must respect reduced-motion preferences;
- consent controls must not hide the principal task.

## Measurement plan

Define events before adding analytics. Do not assign targets until a real baseline exists.

Culinary:

- consumer culinary CTA click;
- dish dossier view;
- recipe, ingredient, tradition, guide, or source continuation;
- culinary search used;
- recipe scaling or cook-mode use when available;
- return to another culinary entity.

Local, only after a verified consumer location launches:

- location viewed;
- hours or access information viewed;
- consumer contact action used;
- current local menu viewed.

Commerce, only after launch:

- category viewed;
- search used;
- product viewed;
- variation selected;
- add to cart;
- cart viewed;
- delivery or pickup context selected;
- checkout started;
- order completed;
- payment failure;
- support or return path used.

Keep consent, retention, data ownership, and event purpose documented.

## Final benchmark position

Complete99 must become a consumer culinary brand in public and remain an internal management system in private. The two roles must not share a landing-page hierarchy, public conversion path, sitemap, or messaging layer.

The public website should first become a small, excellent, evidence-led culinary library for dishes, recipes, ingredients, traditions, and practical guides. Verified consumer locations can follow when real local evidence exists. Commerce can follow only when products, prices, fulfilment, policies, support, and test transactions are real. Institutional, workforce, inventory, supplier, cost, campaign, and operations matters remain exclusively in the authenticated private Complete99 OS.

The near-term experience target is:

- as immediate and local as Hummus Ashkara;
- as commercially complete at item level as Delitlv when commerce launches;
- as atmospheric as R2M;
- as transparent at item level as Sweetgreen;
- as connected across content and commerce as Ottolenghi;
- as navigable at retail scale as Eataly;
- as recognizable on mobile as Farmer J;
- while remaining more truthful, less interruptive, and more accessible than the reviewed benchmarks.
