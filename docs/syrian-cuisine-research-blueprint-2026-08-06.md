# Complete99 Syrian cuisine research blueprint

Last reviewed: 2026-08-07

## Purpose

This document defines the first Syrian cuisine expansion for the Complete99
culinary knowledge and commerce graph. It is private source documentation. It
does not itself publish a page, create a WooCommerce product, claim a supplier
relationship, set stock, or activate an offer.

The consumer promise is simple:

> Syrian cuisine is not one recipe. Aleppo is known for kibbeh and sour-fruit
> sauces, Damascus for stuffed leaves and yogurt dishes, the coast for fish,
> the Euphrates for oven bread and wheat dishes, and the south for distinct
> Mleihi preparations. The guide presents sourced regional and family versions
> without inventing one owner when the evidence does not decide the question.

Public copy must help a person decide what to eat, what to buy, how to cook, or
what to learn next. Terms about registries, migrations, candidates, evidence
classes, deployment and internal workflow remain in administration and source
documentation only.

## Scope

The full foundation contains:

- one Syrian cuisine root;
- nine regional hubs;
- 24 dish entities;
- 46 ingredient entities;
- 12 technique entities;
- eight tradition entities;
- six separate preparation entities: two under the shared Mleihi dish, two
  under the shared Yabraq and Yebra dish, and separate Homs and Hama
  preparations under the contested Halawet al-Jibn owner.

This is an ontology count, not a promise of 106 public URLs. A standalone public
route exists only when it owns a distinct search task, has useful bilingual
content, source coverage, cleared visual media, working continuation actions,
and no conflict with the menu, ingredient, knowledge or WooCommerce owner.

106 culinary ontology entities plus 3 private held market observations = 109
Syrian module entities. Public state after this tranche is 1 safe noindex
gateway plus 108 private entities.

## Evidence model

The existing evidence vocabulary stays authoritative:

- `official_source`: a government, university, museum or institution, limited
  to the claim that source actually supports;
- `third_party_guide`: named participatory oral history or family food archive;
- `peer_reviewed_context`: food science or agriculture context, never a
  laboratory result for a Complete99 recipe, product or lot;
- `regulatory_standard`: food safety, storage, labeling or allergen guidance;
- `market_observation`: an exact product, seller, price, unit and date;
- `editorial_inference`: a navigation or merchandising link, never a historical
  ownership claim.

Editorial grading used during review:

- A: direct institutional or primary evidence, or two independent sources for
  the narrow claim;
- B: a named account supported by another relevant source;
- C: one named person or family account, always published with attribution;
- D: conflicting or unresolved identity, kept private until clarified.

## Ingredient map

The 46 ingredient concepts are bulgur, jareesh, freekeh, rice, dry chickpeas,
green peas, lentils, dry cowpeas, grape leaves, rural Raqqa tannour bread,
matzah, onion, aubergine, chard, red pepper paste, dried red pepper, tomato
paste, pomegranate molasses, lemon, tamarind, sumac, sour cherry, quince,
tahini, walnuts, source-unspecified nuts, pistachios, cheese, semolina, qeshta
or cream, sugar syrup, fresh yogurt, kishk, jameed, higet, haqt, olive oil,
samneh or ghee, lamb or beef, whole chicken, coastal fish, garlic, dried
apricot, allspice, dried mint, and `ou`, retained only as the
souring-concentrate term used by the Aleppan Jewish family source.

Identity boundaries:

- bulgur and jareesh are not aliases;
- green peas in the documented Ouzi are not chickpeas;
- kishk, jameed, higet and haqt are not aliases;
- source-unspecified nuts are not converted to walnuts, pistachios or another
  nut type without direct evidence;
- whole chicken is not absorbed into the lamb-or-beef category;
- the family-source term `ou` is not an alias for tamarind, apricot or
  pomegranate until additional identity evidence defines its composition;
- origin is taken from an exact label or supplier record, never inferred from
  association with a cuisine;
- `Aleppo-style pepper` is used unless exact geographic origin is verified;
- fish requires species, origin and cold-chain identity at SKU level;
- fresh meat and dairy are excluded from the first new commerce wave.

## Source-edge rules

- Every dish-to-ingredient `requires` relation carries only the source IDs and
  evidence class that support that ingredient in that exact dish or
  preparation.
- A dish's general bibliography, community-history source or scientific context
  is not copied to every ingredient relation. General dish sources never fan
  out automatically across the ingredient graph.
- An ingredient-to-dish reverse relation is recorded per documented target and
  per regional source. A national or multi-region ingredient record does not
  lend its aggregated source list to one regional dish.
- Shared canonical owners may hold a sourced identity or comparison fact, but
  they do not merge variant ingredient lists. The exact recipe and production
  specification, or bill of materials (BOM), stays on the preparation entity
  that the source actually documents.
- An ingredient described as optional, occasional or version-specific uses a
  source-bounded `references` relation rather than `requires`. It does not enter
  the required cross-sell list.
- Scientific category context remains a separate fact with its own evidence
  class. It is not evidence for a cultural attribution or for an unrelated
  ingredient edge.

### Corrected source-bounded edges

- Muhammara remains formula-free in this tranche. The Aleppo Project supports
  a bibliography reference and Nariman supports Homs household-mouneh context;
  neither is fanned out into a shared required-ingredient formula.
- Kibbeh Meshwiyyeh links bulgur, red meat and source-unspecified nuts only to
  Mirvet's Aleppo account. It does not convert the source word `nuts` to
  walnuts.
- Damascene Ouzi requires green peas in Razan's account. Chickpeas have no Ouzi
  edge.
- Lahm bi Ajin uses the bounded Jewish Food Society family source for meat,
  onion, pomegranate molasses and tomato paste. Kebab bil Karaz links meat and
  sour cherry only and has no unsupported samn edge.
- The Halawet al-Jibn parent has no required ingredients. The Homs preparation
  links cheese, semolina, qeshta or cream and syrup only to Nariman. The Hama
  preparation links qeshta or cream and pistachios only to Noor, without
  inferring a dough formula.
- The Suwayda Mleihi preparation uses source-unspecified nuts from Ghaimana,
  not walnuts. It remains separate from the Hauran jameed preparation.
- Matzah in Matzah Kebab and whole chicken in Dajaj Mashwi use their exact
  FOODISH recipe sources. Broad community sources do not fan out into those
  dish-to-ingredient edges.
- Sayadiyah and its onion-browning technique link onion to Zainab's coastal
  account. Exact color, time and temperature remain kitchen-test variables.
- Sour-fruit braising keeps cherry and quince contexts separate and carries no
  pomegranate-molasses edge. Scientific fruit literature remains context only.
- Syrian mouneh uses UNESCO and named cultural testimony for cultural edges.
  FDA water-activity guidance remains safety context and is not cultural proof.

## Technique map

1. Bulgur hydration: granulation, water ratio, temperature and rest.
2. Kibbeh shell forming: shell-to-filling mass and shell thickness.
3. Kibbeh cooking: baking, frying or simmering with safe core temperature.
4. Yogurt sauce stability: pH, temperature, mixing and recipe composition.
5. Stuffing grape leaves: leaf size, filling mass and cooking loss.
6. Sour-fruit braising: separate cherry and quince contexts, with pH, Brix and
   sensory balance held for testing and no pomegranate-molasses edge.
7. Charred aubergine: heat, peeling and moisture management.
8. Sayadiyah onion browning: color target, time and heat without burning.
9. Saj bread: dough hydration, thickness and surface temperature.
10. Tannour bread: oven method and high-heat safety.
11. Syrian mouneh: cultural practice is sourced separately from pH, water
    activity and process validation.
12. Cultured dried dairy: microbiology, pH, water activity and shelf life.

Literature values do not become recipe settings. Exact ratios, temperatures,
yield and sensory targets require a Complete99 kitchen trial.

## Tradition map

- Syrian hospitality and food sharing, described by region rather than as one
  universal practice;
- Syrian mouneh, seasons, family transmission and displacement;
- Ramadan and Eid foodways in named Syrian accounts;
- Aleppan Jewish foodways, including Sabbath, Passover and diaspora continuity;
- Damascene Jewish foodways as a distinct branch from Aleppo;
- Syrian Armenian Aleppo foodways, supported by food-specific museum evidence;
- Kurdish Afrin foodways, limited to the named evidence scope;
- Druze Suwayda hospitality and Mleihi, limited to the sourced regional scope.

Demographic evidence alone does not prove a culinary entity. A broad Syrian
Christian, Assyrian or Syriac cuisine entity remains held until food-specific
sources support it.

## Canonical and variant rules

- `dish-mansaf-mleihi` owns the dish intent but carries no merged ingredient
  list or BOM.
- `preparation-mleihi-suwayda-fresh-yogurt` describes the Suwayda preparation.
- `preparation-mleihi-hauran-jameed` describes the Hauran preparation.
- The two Mleihi preparation entities own their exact, separately sourced
  ingredient relations.
- `dish-halawet-al-jibn` records a contested origin and does not select a winner.
  The shared parent carries no required ingredient list.
- `preparation-halawet-homs-cheese-semolina` records Nariman's Homs account
  with cheese, semolina, qeshta or cream and syrup.
- `preparation-halawet-hama-qeshta-pistachio` records Noor's Hama account with
  qeshta or cream and pistachios, without inferring a dough composition.
- The two Halawet preparation entities remain separate, do not decide the city
  of origin, and do not infer fresh yogurt or samn.
- `dish-yabraq-yebra` owns the shared dish question; Damascene Yabraq and
  Aleppan Jewish Yebra are preparations. Yalangi remains a separate dish.
- `preparation-yabraq-damascene` records the sourced meat-and-rice Damascene
  preparation.
- `preparation-yebra-aleppan-jewish-apricot` records the sourced Charles Dabbah
  family preparation with dried apricots and the unresolved source term `ou`.
- kebab bil karaz, lahmeh bi karaz and cherry kebab are term variants for one
  owner.
- lahm b'ajin, lahmacun and sfiha are related terms, not automatic aliases.
- cross-border dishes are described as a sourced regional version, never as
  exclusive national ownership.
- a medieval text proves historical depth, not an unchanged modern recipe.

## Search and publication gates

The intended Syrian hub task is:

`Syrian cuisine by dish, ingredient, region and community`.

Initial sections answer:

- what to eat;
- what to buy for the kitchen;
- how to cook;
- why the flavor works;
- which region, community or family account the version comes from.

No entity becomes indexable merely because it is visible. Index approval needs
one intent owner, complete Hebrew and English, useful original content, dated
sources, cleared media and alt text, reciprocal internal links, a working next
action, canonical and hreflang parity, suitable schema, and no collision with
the menu, ingredient library, knowledge center or store.

A dish or recipe also needs a tested formula, yield, allergen and cross-contact
review, safe temperatures, kitchen approval and useful long-form content. A
product also needs a real SKU, price, stock, fulfillment and a working purchase
flow.
